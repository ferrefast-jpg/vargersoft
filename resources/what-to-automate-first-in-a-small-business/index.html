What should a small business automate first?

Automate first the task that is repeated every day, follows the same steps each time and costs you customers when it is done late. For most small businesses that is answering the phone, following up on quotes or confirming appointments. Fix the process before you automate it, because automating a broken process only makes it fail faster.

Last reviewed: November 17, 2026 · VARGERSOFT

Three questions to find the task

  • Does it happen at least once a day?
  • Could you write its steps on one page?
  • Does a customer wait, or leave, when it is late?

A task with three yes answers is a good first candidate. A task that needs judgment every time is not.

Fix it on paper first

Write the steps as they really happen, not as they should. Remove the ones nobody needs. Decide what the right result looks like. Only then choose a tool.

Set one number and one date

Before you start, pick the number the automation should move, such as missed calls per week or days to collect an invoice, and the date you will check it. If the number has not moved by that date, change the plan.

What to leave alone

Keep people on the moments that build trust: a complaint, a negotiation, bad news. Automation should carry the routine so your team has time for those.
