What is llms.txt, and does your website need it?

llms.txt is a plain Markdown file placed at the root of a website that gives AI systems a short summary of the site and links to its most important pages. It is a proposal from 2024, not an official standard. Studies published in 2026 found that AI crawlers rarely request it, and Google has said its search products do not use it. It costs little to publish, but it will not make an unreadable website readable.

Last reviewed: November 17, 2026 · VARGERSOFT

What goes inside the file

A title, a one-paragraph description of the business, and short lists of links grouped under headings such as Services, Pricing and Contact. A second file, llms-full.txt, can hold the full text of those pages.

What the evidence says

Independent studies in 2026 measured how often AI crawlers ask for the file, and the answer was almost never. One analysis reported that most sites with a valid file received no requests for it in a month. The tools that do read it today are mostly coding assistants working with developer documentation.

Should you publish one?

Yes, if it takes minutes, because a short accurate summary does no harm. Do not pay for it as if it were a ranking tool. Put the real effort into the things assistants demonstrably read: complete HTML pages, clear answers and structured data.

The file that matters more: robots.txt

robots.txt decides which crawlers may read your site at all, and the major AI crawlers follow it. A robots file that blocks them removes you from their answers, whatever your llms.txt says.
